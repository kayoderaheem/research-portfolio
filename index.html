Problem-choice framework

Good projects are designed
before they are executed.

The workflow converts scientific taste into visible choices that can be challenged, improved, and revisited.

01

Generate in parallel

Develop several candidate problems before committing. Compare them while changing direction is still inexpensive.

Avoid first-idea bias
02

Name the objective

Decide what impact means: biological learning, generality, clinical utility, broad use, or an irreplaceable capability.

Make values explicit
03

Fix one anchor

Hold the core question or unique capability fixed. Let the model, dataset, assay, and application compete to serve it.

Prevent method lock-in
04

Map every assumption

Score each scientific and technical assumption by risk and time-to-readout. Long, fragile chains need redesign.

Risk × time
05

Test the weakest link

Run the cheapest discriminating experiment early—even an imperfect prototype—before investing in scale.

Earliest go / no-go
06

Revisit the tree

Alternate focused execution with detached review. A negative result should reveal the strongest next branch.

Earn the right to scale.

The initial signal is promising, but the claim should now survive stronger baselines, leakage-resistant validation, calibration checks, and an independent cohort.

  • Freeze the primary endpoint before expansion.
  • Compare against simple and established methods.
  • Quantify uncertainty and subgroup stability.
  • Seek orthogonal biological evidence.

Make hidden risk visible.

Working system

From intuition to
a recorded decision.

GitHub Issues hold the scientific reasoning. Automation compares mature ideas and republishes the portfolio without hiding the uncertainty.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Write the observation, problem, question, hypothesis, and why it matters.

  2. 02

    Stress-test

    Expose assumptions, competing explanations, data limitations, and failure modes.

  3. 03

    Compare

    Use pairwise review to contrast impact, tractability, evidence, and publication leverage.

  4. 04

    Decide

    Choose continue, refine, pivot, park, or stop—and record what evidence drove the choice.
